# Magellan Flights — Social Posts (newsletter-growth playbook)

**Goal of every post:** give away ONE piece of research → drive a free newsletter sub.
**The CTA (use one):**
- `Full weekly flight-market briefing — free → magellanflights.com/newsletter`
- `I email the week's biggest below-normal drops, free. Link in bio.`

**Voice:** you're an analyst, not a coupon site. Always frame fares as *below their normal price* (you judge each route against its own history). Keep it honest — prices move; live price confirmed on Aviasales. One-way-led.

**Cadence:** 2–3×/week minimum. Repurpose one idea across platforms. Put the link in bio + first comment (links in-post get throttled on IG/FB).

**Image options:** a heatmap screenshot, the OG card, the flyer, or a clean text-on-parchment graphic. Video > image where you can.

# STORM-driven brand content (from the 2026-06-29 defensibility research)

The research found the moat isn't data — it's **trust + per-airport relevance + staying free**. That's not just strategy; it's the best *content* you have. People share honesty and a savings number, not analysis. Use these to build the audience.

## FORMAT H — The transparency brand story (the strongest post you can make)
The single most shareable thing here is you being honest about your own odds. Vulnerability = trust = follows.
> I run a flight-deal site. So I ran deep research on whether it can even survive against Google Flights (which is free and just launched AI deal-finding).
> The honest answer: not as a "data" site — Google won that. It survives only if it does three things the big guys won't:
> 1. Show you deals from **your** airport (the #1 complaint about every deal newsletter).
> 2. Stay **actually free** (the #1 complaint about the paid ones is sneaky billing).
> 3. Tell you the truth about whether a fare is really cheap — and show the math.
> So that's what Magellan is. Free, honest, from your airport. → magellanflights.com/newsletter

## FORMAT I — "Why I'll never paywall the deals"
> Every flight-deal club's worst reviews say the same thing: "they kept billing me after I canceled."
> Magellan's deals are free. Forever. No card, no trial trap.
> If I ever make money it'll be from honest credit-card picks and an optional power-user add-on — never from holding the deals hostage.
> The week's biggest below-normal fares, free → link in bio.

## FORMAT J — "How we know it's actually cheap" (transparency = trust)
> "70% off!" — okay, off of *what*?
> Most deal sites never tell you. We do: we compare the cheapest one-way we've tracked to that route's normal price (from round-trip data + its own history). Bigger gap = better deal. It's a guide, not a guarantee, and you confirm the live price before booking.
> No hype. Just the math. → magellanflights.com/newsletter

## FORMAT K — "From YOUR airport" (the relevance wedge)
Pair the share with a concrete saving — that's the unit people forward.
> Cheap one-ways this week, by where you actually live:
> • From the East Coast: {CITY} ${PRICE} (JFK), {PCT}% below normal
> • From the West Coast: {CITY} ${PRICE} (LAX), {PCT}% below
> • From the middle: {CITY} ${PRICE} (ORD/ATL/DFW), {PCT}% below
> Pick your home airport, get only your deals — free → magellanflights.com

## Why these work (the psychology)
- **Honesty is a flex** competitors can't copy — they're VC-funded and have to upsell; you can tell the truth.
- **The savings number is the shareable unit** ("$52 LAX→Vancouver, 64% below") — lead with it, people forward *it*, not your analysis.
- **"From your airport"** turns a generic deal into a personal one — the #1 thing subscribers say is missing everywhere else.
- Post Format H once as a pinned/anchor post; rotate I/J/K to reinforce the three brand pillars (free · honest · relevant).
