# Magellan Flights — Writing Style Guide (apply to every blog post)

This is the reusable voice template. The goal (per the SEO method): write so people
actually *want to read it* — because dwell time and low bounce are real ranking
signals, and AI slop makes people bounce. If a post reads like a press release,
it fails, no matter how "optimized" it is.

## Who we are (point of view)
A sharp, slightly obsessive flight-deals desk that tracks fares like a stock
market. We're the savvy friend who books a $300 round-trip to Lisbon and won't
shut up about it — not a corporate travel brand. Traveler-to-traveler, never
"valued customer." We have real data (the fares we track every day) and we use it.

## Tone
- **Confident and plainspoken.** Short, punchy sentences mixed with longer ones.
- **Warm and a little cheeky.** A wink, not a clown act. We're fun, but people are
  about to spend real money on travel — earn the joke, then get back to helping.
- **Honest.** We say when something's a myth, when we're unsure, and when a "deal"
  isn't one. Trust is the whole product.
- **Specific.** Real cities, real numbers, real dates. "A flight to Europe" is weak;
  "a $341 round-trip JFK→Lisbon in October" is us.

## The opening (most important sentence on the page)
Never open with throat-clearing. Open with a hook: a contrarian truth, a relatable
moment, or a number. The reader decided whether to stay by the end of sentence one.

- ❌ "In today's fast-paced world, finding cheap flights can be a daunting task."
- ✅ "Everyone 'knows' you should book flights on a Tuesday. It's half true — and
  it's quietly costing you money."

## Hard bans (the AI-slop tells — never use)
- "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're a seasoned traveler or just…"
- "Let's dive in", "Look no further", "the world of travel"
- "It's important to note that", "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion"
- Restating the question as the first sentence ("Many people wonder what the…")
- Empty hedging ("There are many factors to consider")
- Fake precision or made-up stats. If we cite a number, it's real or clearly "roughly".
- Em-dash pileups and three-adjective stacks ("seamless, effortless, stress-free").

## Do
- Write in second person ("you"), active voice.
- **Answer the query in the first 1–2 sentences** (featured-snippet bait), then
  earn the scroll with depth.
- Use our live data as proof: "We track 475 routes and log the price daily…"
- Scannable: short paragraphs, clear H2s, the occasional bolded payoff line.
- One genuine laugh or vivid image per few sections — situational, not forced.
- End sections with a reason to keep reading or a useful next step.
- Every post links to the relevant money page (market / world / last-minute / home).

## Humor calibration (for a money topic)
Light and situational. Think "the price you saw yesterday is already gone — airfare
has the object permanence of a toddler," not a string of puns. If a joke would make
a reader trust us *less* with their $900 booking, cut it.

## Structure we reuse
1. Hook + answer (snippet bait)
2. "The real story" / myth-vs-reality
3. The lever that actually matters (with our data)
4. Practical how-to (numbered, skimmable)
5. CTA to the live board
6. FAQ (the secondary keywords as natural questions)

## Before / after (the standard to hit)
- **Slop:** "There are several factors that determine the best time to book an
  international flight. Booking in advance is generally recommended."
- **Us:** "Book an international flight too early and you overpay for the airline's
  peace of mind. Too late and you're funding their yacht. The sweet spot is wider
  than people think — here's where it actually sits."

## Byline & POV
Posts are published as **Magellan Flights** ("we"), not a personal author. Keep the
"we track / we've seen / our board" framing — it's our credibility.
